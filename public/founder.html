Founder profile

Vishwajeet Adkine

Founder & Lead, Think 42 AI Labs

Vishwajeet Adkine is an AI student, developer and the Founder of Think 42 AI Labs, an independent student-led initiative. He is interested in building useful AI systems—from machine learning models and language-model applications to software that addresses problems beyond the screen. His approach is shaped by experimentation, model evaluation and a belief that advanced technology should be useful in everyday settings.

About

His interest in artificial intelligence is driven by the gap between what modern AI can do and what people can practically access. Through Think 42 AI Labs, he is developing a student-led community for learning, technical exploration and collaborative projects. The initiative takes inspiration from research-oriented institutions such as MBZUAI, particularly their focus on advancing AI research and translating it into real-world applications. Think 42 AI Labs is independent and has no institutional affiliation with MBZUAI.

Rather than presenting every initiative as a finished product, his work is an ongoing process of prototyping, testing, documenting and improving. This includes exploring model adaptation for domain-specific tasks, developing learning resources, and building software experiences around practical user needs.

He is particularly interested in the intersection of machine learning, accessible interfaces and problems that affect communities directly.

A vision for agricultural AI

A major motivation behind his work is agriculture. Through CeryS AI, he is exploring how AI can become a practical assistant for farmers—not simply a tool that identifies a plant disease, but a more connected source of timely, understandable and locally relevant guidance.

The longer-term vision is to bring together plant-health analysis, crop and cultivation guidance, weather context, information about relevant government schemes, and conversational support in regional languages. The goal is to reduce the distance between technical knowledge and the decisions farmers make in the field.

This is an evolving product vision. It requires careful model evaluation across real farm conditions, reliable information, accessible design and feedback from farmers. AI should support informed decisions, not replace agricultural experts or guarantee a diagnosis.

Technical interests & capabilities

His hands-on work spans several parts of the AI development process, from data preparation and experimentation to application integration and evaluation.

Machine learning: classification workflows, model training, performance evaluation and error analysis.

Applied ML: experience experimenting with XGBoost, image-classification models and explainability methods such as SHAP.

Language models: exploring prompt design, domain adaptation, fine-tuning workflows and evaluation of smaller LLMs.

Programming: Python for AI experimentation, alongside JavaScript and application-level development.

AI applications: integrating model and vision APIs into user-facing tools and conversational experiences.

Product development: Flutter-based application work, API integration, backend workflows and iterative testing.

These are active areas of practice. He continues to strengthen the mathematical, statistical and engineering foundations behind the systems he builds.
